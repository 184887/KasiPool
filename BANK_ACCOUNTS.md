# KasiPool – Group bank accounts (Role 1)

**Question:** can a pool of township businesses open a bank account they own together, so the money never sits with KasiPool or one person?

**Short answer:** yes, in principle. Every big SA bank has a **stokvel account** built for exactly this: a group saving together, run by several signatories, with low or no monthly fees. Researched online on 22 Oct 2026. **Check the details with the bank before opening one**: terms change, and some sources disagree.

## Comparison

| | FNB Stokvel Account | Standard Bank Society Scheme | Absa Grocery Stokvel | Nedbank Stokvel | Capitec Stokvel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Minimum members** | 3 | 5 | 2 | not stated | ? |
| **Signatories** | exactly 3, chosen by members | 3–4 | at least 2 | 3 must go to the branch | ? |
| **Who approves payments** | not stated | **3 signatories approve every payment** in the app | not stated | not stated | ? |
| **Opening deposit** | none | R100 | R50 | R100 | ? |
| **Monthly fee** | none (transaction fees apply) | none only if balance stays above R10,000 | none | none | ? |
| **How to open** | online, each signatory on their own phone | in branch | not stated | in branch | ? |
| **Documents** | IDs, proof of address | ID (or passport), proof of address <3 months | IDs + proof of address of signatories | IDs, proof of address, **stokvel constitution**, proof of stokvel address | ? |
| **Foreign nationals** | allowed | passport accepted | not stated | not stated | ? |
| **Notes** | Dominant stokvel bank | Payments need 3 approvals: nobody can take the money alone | Built for grocery groups | Offers grocery discounts | **Launched 10 Sept 2026**, details not yet found |

## What this means for KasiPool

**Good news**
- **The trust problem has a ready-made answer.** Standard Bank's rule that 3 signatories approve every payment means no single person can run off with the money. That's exactly what "without trusting anyone with their money" needs.
- **Most accounts have no monthly fee**, so a pool costs nothing to keep open.
- **Banks are competing hard for stokvels.** The market is about 800,000 stokvels with 11 million members, and Capitec has just entered it. Owners may already know how stokvel accounts work, which helps adoption.

**Things to check with a bank (phone or branch)**
1. **Is it allowed for business stock?** These are personal savings accounts for people saving together. Would a bank accept a group of *business owners* using one to buy stock? Ask before promising it in V1.
2. **Can the account pay a wholesaler directly by EFT?** None of the pages say so clearly. Standard Bank mentions payments through the app; Absa mentions EFT payouts.
3. **What does a cash deposit cost?** Many owners deal in cash. FNB's (2020–21) guide charged about R1.10 per R100 at an ATM and R80 + R2.50 per R100 at a branch. **At ~1–2.5% that could eat half the 2.5–5% bulk saving.** Paying in by EFT or app is far cheaper.
4. **Do we need a constitution?** Nedbank asks for one. We could write a simple KasiPool pool constitution template that every pool uses.

## Recommendation

- **For a first test pool: FNB.** No monthly fee, no opening deposit, opened online, 3 members is enough, foreign nationals allowed.
- **If trust is the main worry: Standard Bank**, because every payment needs 3 approvals. But it needs 5 members, a branch visit, and the fee is only waived above R10,000, which a pool that spends its money weekly won't keep.
- **Make paying in by EFT or app the default**, and keep cash deposits as a backup. Cash fees can wipe out the saving.
- **KasiPool idea:** write a one-page pool constitution template (members, signatories, how orders and payments are approved, what happens if someone leaves). It makes opening the account faster and shows owners the rules up front.
