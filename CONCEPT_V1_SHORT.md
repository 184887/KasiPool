---

**KasiPool helps small township businesses buy stock together, so they get bulk prices without trusting anyone with their money.**

## Problem
Township businesses need the same stock from the same wholesalers, but local owners buy alone. Each buys too little for bulk prices, which at the wholesaler we interviewed only apply to full pallets. On top of that, they pay for transport and close the shop to fetch stock. Standard Bank's 2025 township research names "the absence of group buying benefits" and stock transport as common challenges. Meanwhile, foreign-owned shops buy together in small organised groups and get the volume price. Local bulk buying is "minimal" because "they're not working together" (wholesaler interview).

## Customer
Small, South African-owned township businesses that buy similar stock, starting with spaza shops in Philippi, Cape Town, then taverns, salons and food sellers. Spazas are 10–20% of township micro-enterprises; Browns Farm in Philippi alone has around 170. Later, other groups with a shared buying need could pool too, such as students in residences.

## What we learned from testing
We spoke to [TODO: number] people near a cash and carry and to staff at a wholesaler.
- **We were wrong that locals won't cooperate.** People told us locals are happy to come together to make money.
- **Group buying already happens,** but local groups run informally on handshakes and Facebook, purely on trust, so they stay small.
- **The bulk saving is 2.5–5%,** modest but large for businesses with ~20% margins.
- **The wholesaler welcomed** the idea of more local bulk customers.

## Solution
About 10 nearby businesses form a **pool** and order through **WhatsApp** every two weeks. Each pays its share into a **stokvel bank account the pool owns**: an existing product at every big SA bank, where several signatories must approve payments. KasiPool combines the orders into one bulk order with a partner wholesaler, who delivers to the pool. A trusted pool captain checks each business's share. **The money never passes through KasiPool or one person.**

## Value
For a typical spaza buying R12,000/month of staples through a pool: **R820–R1,120 saved per month (+16–22% profit)** if the wholesaler delivers free, or R220–R520 if the pool pays for a shared drop point [estimates]. Owners also save the trip to the cash and carry. Wholesalers get steady new bulk customers.

## Competition
Yebo Fresh (Cape Town) and Vuleka (Johannesburg) sell stock to spazas from their own warehouses. **KasiPool is different:** businesses buy together straight from wholesalers, and keep control of their money. KasiPool needs no warehouse and carries no stock.

## Business model
Wholesalers pay KasiPool a ~1.5% commission on pooled orders [estimate]. Businesses pay KasiPool nothing. 100 spazas ≈ R1.2m/month in orders ≈ R18,000/month for KasiPool.

## Biggest risks we'll test next
1. **Trust in the group account:** will owners pay into an account shared with other businesses?
2. **Bank rules:** may business owners use a stokvel account to buy stock? Cash deposit fees could eat the saving.
3. **Wholesaler terms:** will they give pools the bulk price, deliver free and pay commission?
4. **Competition:** do owners already use Yebo Fresh?

## Next steps
Call a bank and 3 wholesalers, talk to more owners (including taverns and salons), and run one test pool of about 10 shops on WhatsApp.
