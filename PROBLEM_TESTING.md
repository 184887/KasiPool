# KasiPool – Problem Testing Log

Evidence gathered to test our assumptions for Concept V1.

## Assumptions

| # | Assumption | Status | Evidence |
|---|------------|--------|----------|
| A1 | Township retailers compete and don't want to see each other win, so they won't cooperate on buying | **Disproved** | Interviews 1, 2+ |
| A2 | Retailers would save money by buying in bulk together | **Supported, but savings are small (~2.5–5%)** | Wholesaler 1 |
| A3 | Retailers would pay in advance and a delivery fee | Untested. Risk: the fee must be smaller than the 2.5–5% saving | Wholesaler 1 |
| A4 | Group buying is already happening, so the behaviour exists | **Supported (many sources)** | Interviews 2+ |
| A5 | Retailers would trust a formal platform instead of personal relationships | Untested (key risk) | |
| A6 | Local spazas buy too little to get wholesale prices, and transport makes small trips expensive | **Supported** | Wholesaler 1 |
| A7 | Wholesalers would welcome pooled local orders | **Supported (one wholesaler)** | Wholesaler 1 |
| A8 | Foreign-owned networks would use KasiPool | **Doubtful**: they already buy together with no friction | Wholesaler 1 |

## Interviews

### Interview 1 – 21 Oct 2026, outside the cash and carry
- **Who:** A local woman (informal trader / shopper, role to confirm)
- **What she said:**
  - She sees a clear benefit in the project.
  - When it comes to business, locals are willing to come together. Everyone is happy to make money.
- **What it changes:** We assumed competition would stop retailers from cooperating (A1). She says the opposite: making money matters more than rivalry.
- **Caveat:** This is one person. Interviews 2+ below back up the cooperation point.

### Interviews 2+ – 21 Oct 2026, several people (number and roles to confirm)
- **What they said:**
  - Many shops already buy together, especially foreign-owned ones (Somali, Bangladeshi).
  - Some local owners buy together too, but only informally: handshake deals, informal arrangements or Facebook groups. Nothing is formal, and it all runs on trust.
- **What it means for KasiPool:**
  - Group buying already happens (A4), so we don't need to invent the habit. We need to make it easier and safer.
  - Foreign-owned networks show the model works, and they are a competitor or benchmark for local spazas.
  - Local group buying is limited by trust and informality. KasiPool's value could be formalising that trust: payments held safely, verified orders, QR-confirmed delivery.
- **Strength:** The same picture came from many people, not one.

### Wholesaler 1 – 21 Oct 2026, staff at a hybrid wholesale/retail store
- **Source:** recording `Wholesaler 1.m4a`, transcript in `Interviews/`. The audio is noisy, so the details below should be checked against the recording.
- **How their wholesale works:**
  - You count as a bulk customer when you buy full pallets (e.g. pallets of rice or sugar). There's no registration, you just come and buy.
  - The wholesale saving is about **2.5–5%**. Margins are thin. Big customers spend roughly R100k to R1m a day.
  - They won't share a price list (they worry we could be a competitor).
  - They run a **WhatsApp group for business customers** that announces specials (e.g. R50 instead of R70), and they offer online ordering and some local delivery. Security is a major issue for deliveries.
- **Somali (and Bangladeshi, Chinese) networks:**
  - Organised groups of roughly 10–15 people, sometimes one person owning 5–10 shops, buying together to get the volume price.
  - Based on an informal agreement. Some kind of pooled deposit / voucher system was mentioned (unclear in the recording).
  - Not one big cabal, but many small groups.
  - Owners work very long hours and often sleep in the shop.
- **Local spazas:**
  - Very few local owners buy wholesale, because they don't work together.
  - Individual spazas need only one or two units of a product. That's not enough volume, and transport for a small trip is expensive.
- **What it means for KasiPool:**
  - The core problem is confirmed by a supplier: locals miss out on volume prices because they buy alone, and transport costs make it worse.
  - The wholesaler would welcome more local customers, so wholesalers are possible partners.
  - **Big risk:** the saving is only 2.5–5%. KasiPool's delivery fee and margin have to fit inside that, or the transport saving has to make up the difference. This needs numbers.
  - Foreign-owned groups probably won't need KasiPool, because they already do this without friction. Our customer is the local spaza owner.
  - Idea: KasiPool could watch wholesaler specials (like their WhatsApp group) and pool orders around them for a bigger saving.
- **Follow-up:** get the wholesaler's name/store and role, and ask about minimum order size, delivery cost and whether they'd give a pooled group a bulk price.

## Next questions to ask
- When an informal group buy goes wrong, what happens? (Who loses money, how often?)
- How do the Facebook groups work: who organises, how do people pay, how is stock split?
- How do the Somali and Bangladeshi networks buy, and why does it work for them?
- Would local owners trust an app they don't know with money upfront? What would make them trust it?
- What would make you trust a group order: who holds the money, who you know in the group?
- Where do you buy stock today, how often, and how do you get it to your shop?
- Would you pay upfront for a cheaper price delivered to your shop? How much of a fee is OK?
