## Brand overview

What KasiPool is, how a purchase moves through it, and what the first release covers.

#### The business Confirmed

KasiPool is a managed collective purchasing platform for Cape Town townships, including Khayelitsha, Gugulethu, Langa, Nyanga, Philippi, Delft, Mitchells Plain, Crossroads, Manenberg and Bonteheuwel. It helps small local businesses combine demand for stock so they can reach bulk purchasing opportunities together. KasiPool selects products, arranges supplier offers, aggregates demand, communicates with buyers and coordinates purchasing and fulfilment.

It is being developed through UCT MakersForge and is an early-stage commercial venture.

#### The central idea Confirmed

Individual businesses. Combined buying power.

Each owner keeps their own shop and their own decisions. Joining a pool adds their quantity to a larger, coordinated purchase.

### The buyer journey Confirmed

1. **Browse**Curated catalogue and active offers.
2. **Choose**Product, pack size, price, minimum, deadline, fulfilment.
3. **Join**Say how many units you want.
4. **Pool**Your quantity joins the shared total.
5. **Confirm**KasiPool checks the conditions when the offer closes.
6. **Pay and procure**Agreed payment, then supplier purchase.
7. **Receive**Collect or receive your allocation.

**The line the whole identity protects:** an expression of interest is not a paid, confirmed order. Steps 3 and 4 are interest. Money and commitment begin only after step 5. Every screen, message and badge in this manual keeps those two things visibly apart.

### Business model and first release

#### Revenue Assumption

KasiPool aims to earn revenue for organising and managing pooled purchases. The mechanism is not chosen: a disclosed service fee, a trading margin or a supplier commission are all open. This manual never states a mechanism or percentage as agreed.

Recommendation Whichever model is chosen, the buyer sees one total payable, with every charge itemised before they join and again before they pay.

#### MVP boundaries Confirmed

- Mobile-first web app and a limited, curated catalogue
- Selected offers with quantities and deadlines
- Quantity-based participation with visible pooling progress
- Basic buyer registration and order tracking
- Admin interface for offers and participants
- Human coordination behind the scenes
- WhatsApp sharing, updates or support where useful

**Not to be presented as existing:** credit, wallets, escrow, automated supplier marketplaces or sophisticated logistics. Nothing in this manual, including the mock-ups, depends on them.

## Brand strategy

All wording in this chapter is a recommendation for the team to adopt, edit or reject.

#### Purpose

Give small businesses the same buying leverage as larger ones, without asking them to give up their independence.

#### Mission

Organise pooled purchases that small retailers can understand, join with confidence and track from offer to collection.

#### Vision

Pooled buying becomes a normal, trusted part of how neighbourhood businesses restock.

### Audiences and what they need

| Audience | Practical needs | What the brand must show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Spaza shop and neighbourhood retail owners** (primary) | Predictable stock at a workable cost, less time and transport spent on buying trips, clarity on what they owe and when. | The exact product, pack, price, deadline and total, in plain words, on a small phone screen. |
| **Other small Cape Town township businesses** (hospitality, salons, tuck shops, caterers) | Consumables bought regularly, in quantities that suit a small operation. | That the catalogue is curated for their needs and the pack sizes are stated. |
| **Suppliers and wholesalers** | A credible counterpart with firm quantities, a clear process and a single point of contact. | A concise, professional introduction and consistent documents. |
| **KasiPool operators** (internal) | Fast offer set-up, participant lists, status changes and messages that stay consistent. | A tidy admin interface and reusable message templates. |

#### Positioning statement

For small businesses in Cape Town townships that buy stock in small, separate orders, KasiPool is the managed purchasing platform that brings their orders together into one coordinated purchase, with every quantity, charge and deadline stated up front.

#### Customer value proposition

Join a pool for stock you already sell, see exactly what you would pay, and let KasiPool handle supplier coordination. You may save time and transport, and you can judge for yourself whether the offer is good value.

### Brand promise and the evidence it needs

“You will always see the full picture before you commit.”

| Promise component | Evidence the business must be able to show |
| --- | --- |
| Every offer states product, pack size, price, fees, minimum, deadline and fulfilment. | A required-fields check in the admin tool that blocks publishing an incomplete offer. |
| Buyers know what has and has not happened. | Status labels tied to real events. “Payment confirmed” is set only when a person has verified payment. |
| Outcomes are communicated, including bad ones. | A message template and a send log for closed-below-target and delay cases. |
| Charges are disclosed. | Itemised totals on screen and in messages, once the revenue model is chosen. |

**What the brand does not promise:** guaranteed savings, the lowest prices, risk-free transactions or guaranteed fulfilment. Benefits are phrased as possibilities the model can support: “may reduce”, “when the pool closes at target”, “subject to supplier confirmation”.

### Brand values

#### Show the whole picture

Price, fees, deadline and fulfilment appear together. Nothing important sits behind a tap.

#### Strength in numbers

Frame every offer around what the group does together, in quantities and businesses, not slogans.

#### Respect the owner’s time

Short sentences, one action per screen, no jargon. Business owners are busy and capable.

#### Say what has happened

Report real status. Do not announce a result before it is true.

#### Local without costume

Rooted in South African trade through real products, real places and real language, not borrowed slang or decoration.

### Personality

KasiPool sounds and looks like a well-run trading partner who knows the neighbourhood. Five traits, each with a boundary:

| Trait | Looks and sounds like | Never becomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Commercially competent** | Exact figures, tidy tables, calm confidence | Corporate coldness or jargon |
| **Collectively strong** | “Together we have 38 of 50 cases” | Hype about “the movement” |
| **Accessible** | Plain British English, large tap targets, short screens | Childish or over-simplified |
| **Transparent** | Itemised totals, stated deadlines, explained delays | Over-explaining every step |
| **Locally relevant** | Familiar products, pack sizes and place names | Exaggerated slang, charity framing, poverty imagery |

### Brand story

A spaza owner buys oil, sugar and flour in the quantities their shop can carry. So does the owner two streets away, and the one beyond. Each pays for their own trip and negotiates alone.

KasiPool brings those orders together. We choose the products, line up supplier offers and show everyone the same numbers. When enough businesses join, we coordinate one larger purchase and tell each buyer what happens next.

Every shop stays independent. The buying is shared.

### Tagline options

#### A Recommended

Individual businesses. Combined buying power.

Already the central idea. Names both halves of the model, works as a headline, and makes no claim about price.

#### B

Buy together. Stay independent.

Shorter and works well on WhatsApp and social. Less specific about the commercial benefit.

#### C

Your orders, joined up.

Plain and practical. Weaker on collective strength and easy to misread as a delivery service.

### Primary logo

![Primary KasiPool logo](assets/logo.jpg)

The primary logo is the horizontal lockup exactly as supplied: navy wordmark with the ornate infinity motif standing in for the double “o” of “Pool”. The name is always written **KasiPool**, with capital K and capital P, as one word.

**Proposed interpretation** Recommendation The infinity form can be read as connected participation, continuity and shared opportunity: many buyers, one purchase. This is a brand reading only. No cultural origin or traditional meaning is claimed for the pattern.

The radiating sun-like elements add warmth and a sense of something opening. They are part of the artwork and must not be removed, recoloured or redrawn in the primary logo.

### Backgrounds

Use on

Warm paper `#F5F4EF`, white `#FFFFFF`, and light sand `#EDE6D6`. The navy wordmark sits at 14.1:1 on paper.

Dark backgrounds

Navy text vanishes on dark. Until the reversed vector exists, place the supplied logo on a paper-coloured plate, as on this page. Then use the reversed version below.

Avoid

Photographs, patterns, gradients and any background in gold, orange or brown, where the motif disappears.

### Clear space

Keep a margin of **x** on every side, where **x** is half the height of the capital “K”. Nothing else, including text, edges and other logos, enters that margin.

### Does the ornament survive at small size?

**Assessment:** no, not in full. The infinity silhouette and the warm gold and brown colouring remain recognisable down to about 32 px. The fine pattern bands, dotted rings and ray tips blur together below that. At 16 to 24 px the original motif turns into an unclear brown blob.

Recommendation Use the original motif only inside the full logo at 120 px or wider. For everything smaller, use the simplified companion symbol.

#### Brand colour is not status colour

Gold, ember and umber belong to the brand. They decorate, highlight and show pool progress. They never mean “done”.

Green appears only after a verified event: payment confirmed, collected, completed. A gold progress bar that reaches 100% shows *interest*, and it stays gold.

#### Colour is never alone

Every status carries an icon and a text label as well as colour. Greyscale and colour-blind users read the same state. See the Status chapter.

Warning amber `#8F5200` is deliberately darker and browner than brand gold, so they do not blur.

Two free families, both on Google Fonts, chosen for small-screen clarity and strong numerals.

Aa 0123456789

A solid grotesque in heavy weights. It sits close to the wordmark’s bold, plain letterforms without copying them.

Aa 0123456789

`'Helvetica Neue', Arial, sans-serif`

Used while fonts load or if blocked. WhatsApp images and email use system fonts or outlined text.

Font licences: both families are open-licence (SIL OFL) and free for commercial use. Self-host them in the product to avoid a third-party request on slow connections. Wordmark letterforms stay as supplied.

## Supporting graphics

A restrained set, informed by the logo, whose main job is to show many contributions forming one whole.

#### The unit grid Signature graphic

One square per case. Filled gold squares are cases requested; outlined squares are cases still needed. It shows the pool target at a glance, and a buyer’s own cases can be marked with a navy outline.

38 of 50 cases requested. Illustrative. Use it where the offer has 100 units or fewer. Above that, use the bar.

#### The progress bar

A gold fill with a diagonal hatch and an umber end-cap. The hatch makes it distinguishable without colour and signals “requested”, not “paid”. The target is always written in text beside it.

38 of 50 cases requested 76%

Rule: the bar uses gold at every stage until the offer is confirmed. Only status badges turn green.

#### Pattern bands

Dotted band, from the logo’s dotted rings. For card tops, email headers and social frames. Height 24 to 40 px.

Chevron band, from the ray motif. Use only at the edge of a layout.

#### Watermark motif

The companion symbol at 12 to 20% opacity, cropped off the corner of a hero panel. Never behind prices, quantities or body copy.

#### Icons

Outline icons, 2 px stroke, rounded joins, on a 16 or 24 px grid. Product icons are generic (bottle, sack, case), never a branded pack. Pair every icon with a text label.

**Illustration rules.** Flat shapes in brand colours with umber outlines. Show goods and trading places, not people in need. Decoration stays at the edges and behind nothing a buyer must read. If a graphic competes with a price or a deadline, remove the graphic.

## Photography

Authentic, dignified and specific. No photography was supplied, so these are direction frames, not final images.

Owner at their own counter, mid-task, in natural light

Stock close-up: real shelves, real packs, in the shop’s own arrangement

Two traders comparing a list or phone together, candid

The trading environment: street, signage, stoep, stockroom

#### Do

- Show owners as skilled professionals at work: counting stock, serving, deciding.
- Photograph in the owner’s real shop, in daylight, at eye level.
- Show actual products and pack sizes the catalogue sells.
- Use warm, natural colour that sits with the navy and gold palette.
- Use local photographers and agree a fair fee and usage terms.

#### Avoid

- Poverty imagery, pity framing, empty shelves, children as props.
- Staged handshake or thumbs-up shots and stock “Africa” cliches.
- Faces looking at the camera with a donated-goods feeling.
- Heavy filters, sepia and dramatic sunsets.
- Showing competitor brands or readable customer details.

#### Consent and dignity checklist Recommendation

- Written, informed consent from every identifiable person, in a language they understand.
- Say where the image may appear: app, WhatsApp, social, print, supplier decks.
- Separate consent for each use, with a simple way to withdraw it.
- Photograph only the owner’s own premises, with the owner’s agreement.
- No images of customers or children without a guardian’s consent.
- Show owners the selected images before publishing.
- Keep a signed release log linked to each file.
- Do not use any image to imply an endorsement, testimonial or partnership that does not exist.

## Status system

Nine states for an offer or an order. Each has a distinct icon, a plain label and a colour, so no state depends on colour alone.

Open for interest

People can join and change their quantity.

Info blue · open circle. Applies to the offer and to the buyer’s order. Not paid.

Target reached, awaiting confirmation

The minimum has been requested. KasiPool will check the conditions.

Info blue · half-filled circle. Still not a purchase. Gold progress does not turn green.

Closed below target

The minimum was not met. No purchase will be made.

Neutral grey · circle with minus. Neutral, not red: it is an outcome, not an error.

Awaiting payment

You are confirmed. Payment is due by the stated time.

Warning amber-brown · clock. The only state that asks the buyer to act with money.

Payment confirmed

KasiPool has verified your payment.

Success green · circle with tick. Set only after a person checks.

Procurement in progress

KasiPool is buying from the supplier.

Info blue · exchange arrows. Work underway, not done.

Ready for collection

Your goods are ready at the collection point. Use “Dispatched” if delivery is offered.

Success green · box. Text varies by fulfilment type.

Completed

You have received your goods.

Solid green · filled tick. The only solid badge, so it reads as final.

Cancelled

The offer or order was cancelled. Reason shown on the order.

Error red · slashed circle. Use grey “Closed below target” for an unmet pool, red only for cancellation.

#### How the three signals work together

- **Text:** the label is always written in full, never just a coloured dot.
- **Icon:** each state has its own shape, readable in greyscale: open circle, half circle, minus, clock, tick, arrows, box, filled tick, slash.
- **Colour:** reinforces the group (blue in progress, amber needs action, green confirmed, grey ended, red cancelled).
- **Weight:** badges use 700 weight at 12px, and Completed alone is solid.

#### Rules Recommendation

- Offer status and order status are separate. A buyer’s order shows its own badge on top of the offer’s.
- Never show green before payment is confirmed. “Target reached” is blue.
- Cancelled and Closed below target explain what happens to money, in one sentence.
- Show status in the same spot on every card, top right.

**Assumption:** this sequence assumes buyers pay after the pool is confirmed and before KasiPool buys from the supplier. Whether payment, cancellation and refunds work this way is an operational decision. If they change, the states change with them.

## Voice and messaging

Plain British English, short sentences, specific actions. Confident without claiming certainty.

#### Confident

State what is known. Give the date, the amount, the next step.

#### Respectful

Speak to a business owner as a peer. No pity, no cheerleading.

#### Practical

One idea per sentence. Verbs first on buttons: Join, Pay, Collect.

**Enthusiasm is not certainty.** “The pool has reached its target” means requests have reached the minimum. It does not mean KasiPool has bought anything, that payment is in, or that goods are ready. Pair every positive milestone with what has not yet happened.

### Writing rules

#### Do

- Write “cases”, “packs” and “businesses”. Use the unit every time.
- Use dates as **Fri 30 Oct, 17:00**.
- Say what the buyer should do next.
- Use “may” for savings and “expected” for dates.

#### Avoid

- “Lowest prices”, “guaranteed”, “risk-free”, “huge savings”.
- “Empower communities” and other generic claims.
- Slang or township-flavoured jargon used for effect.
- Exclamation marks, except in rare, clearly warranted cases.

A pool is a shared order. You tell us how many cases you want. Other shops do the same. If enough of us join before the deadline, KasiPool buys the stock in one go. Joining shows interest. It is not a payment, and you pay only if the pool is confirmed.

### Localisation Recommendation

1. **Let the pilot decide the languages.** Ask pilot buyers which language they read business messages in, and start with English plus the one or two languages they name most.
2. **Use professional translators with native review.** Do not machine-translate customer-facing text without review. Have a second native speaker check money, dates and status wording.
3. **Keep a glossary** for pool, offer, interest, confirmed, collection, and the status labels, agreed with translators and tested with buyers.
4. **Write translatable strings.** Full sentences, with variables for amounts and dates, never concatenated fragments.
5. **Keep numbers consistent** across languages: rand format, 24-hour times, the unit beside every quantity.
6. **Test on WhatsApp** in each language, because text length and line breaks change card and message layouts.
